# Designing "Anchor": Prior Art & Behavioral-Science Evidence Review

## TL;DR
- **Anchor's individual ingredients are each well-supported, but the specific combination is genuinely novel.** Anti-streak tracking, decision-removal (implementation intentions), physical-task anchors, and momentum chaining are all backed by strong behavioral science and are already partially implemented by leaders like Finch (shame-free tracking) and Tiimo/Structured (visual day timelines). No shipping app combines a *within-day spatial "path" metaphor* + *rest-as-first-class-object* + *never-forced momentum stack*.
- **The single most important evidence-backed priority is decision-removal via if-then planning** (implementation intentions; Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) found d=.65 on goal attainment across 94 tests and d=.77 for preventing derailment of goal striving) fused with **behavioral activation** ("action precedes motivation"; Cuijpers, van Straten & Warmerdam (2007) found activity scheduling produced a large pooled effect of d=0.87 vs. controls) — these are the mechanisms that actually get low-motivation users to start, and they map directly onto Anchor's locked-the-night-before path.
- **Explicitly avoid rigid streak chains that reset to zero** — the dominant pattern in habit apps — because they trigger the abstinence-violation/"what-the-hell" effect and drive dropout precisely in the population Anchor targets. Anchor's rolling-window/total-count tracking is the correct, evidence-consistent choice.

## Key Findings

### (a) Borrow directly — already solved well elsewhere
1. **Shame-free, no-penalty tracking + warm non-judgmental tone (Finch).** Finch is the category benchmark for the low-motivation audience: no penalty for missed days, a guilt-free option to pause a streak, and a patient companion who's "glad to see you back." Borrow the *tone* and the *return-friendly* framing wholesale.
2. **Visual proportional day-timeline (Structured, Tiimo, TimeBloc).** Structured renders the day as a vertical timeline where each task is a colored block proportional to duration (a 30-minute task takes half the space of an hour); empty space reads as free time, and back-to-back tasks read immediately as crowded. Tiimo adds icon/color coding, gentle prompts, AI task-breakdown, and is built explicitly for executive dysfunction and time-blindness. Anchor's path is a re-skin of this proven "make time visible" paradigm — borrow the proportional-duration rendering and drag-to-reschedule.
3. **Micro-first / "just start" framing (Tiny Habits, 2-minute rule).** Fogg's B=MAP model (Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt) and immediate celebration ("Shine") are the standard for lowering activation energy. Borrow the "make the first step ridiculously small" onboarding and an optional immediate-celebration moment on completion.
4. **AI/assisted task breakdown for overwhelm (Tiimo).** When a task feels too big to start, decomposition into concrete steps with time estimates is now table-stakes for this audience.

### (b) Evidence-backed — prioritize as features
1. **Decision-removal = implementation intentions (if-then planning).** Gollwitzer & Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis (94 independent tests, >8,000 participants, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*) found implementation intentions had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d=.65, 95% CI 0.6–0.7), with an even larger effect (d=.77) on preventing the derailment of goal striving. The 2024 update (Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, 642 independent tests) frames the effect as ranging .27≤d≤.66 depending on plan format and motivational context, with larger effects when plans use a contingent if-then format, the person is motivated, and the plan is rehearsed. Effects are *strongest precisely when self-regulatory problems (failing to get started, getting derailed) beset goal striving* — Anchor's exact users. Locking the path the night before operationalizes this: it pre-commits the cue (time/place) to the response, bypassing in-the-moment negotiation.
2. **Behavioral activation (BA) / activity scheduling.** BA is a first-line, heavily-evidenced treatment for depression; the core insight — "action precedes motivation," reversing the withdrawal→low-mood→withdrawal spiral by scheduling and doing activities despite low energy — is Anchor's founding premise. Cuijpers, van Straten & Warmerdam's 2007 meta-analysis (16 studies, 780 subjects, *Clinical Psychology Review*) found activity scheduling produced a large pooled effect (d=0.87, 95% CI 0.60–1.15) vs. controls, statistically indistinguishable from cognitive therapy (difference d=0.02). Digital BA works: apps including Moodivate, Actify!, Maummove and B-ACT show significant symptom reductions. Critically, the B-ACT trial found that *engagement in emotionally positive activities, not overall activity volume,* was the most consistent predictor of symptom reduction — supporting Anchor's bias toward meaningful/physical anchor tasks over raw task count.
3. **Physical tasks as anchors/starters.** Behavioral-momentum research (the "high-probability request sequence" from ABA) supports front-loading easy, reinforcing actions to build compliance momentum toward harder tasks. Physical tasks (cooking, exercise) give visible proof of mastery and mood lift; Anchor's prioritization of them as starters is defensible.
4. **Momentum stack = activation energy + behavioral momentum.** The "starting is the hardest part; momentum builds once begun" phenomenon is well-described via the activation-energy model and neural-pathway strengthening (the second page is easier than the first). Offering — never forcing — a next task at the moment capacity is elevated is a sound instantiation, but keep it optional to preserve autonomy (self-determination theory).
5. **Protected "least-bad-hour" anchor slot.** Consistent with BA guidance to schedule mood-lifting activities in each person's optimal energy window, and with Tiny Habits' anchoring of a new behavior to a reliable existing cue.
6. **Rest as scheduled, proactive, first-class.** Activity-pacing / energy-envelope research (chronic fatigue/pain) strongly supports *pre-emptive* scheduled rest (vs. reactive rest after a crash) to avoid boom-bust cycles — one first-person recovery account describes scheduled pre-emptive rest as "the most important strategy I used in my recovery." Rendering rest as a visible, legitimate segment operationalizes "rest is a tool, not a reward."

### (c) Explicitly avoid — patterns shown to backfire
1. **Rigid streak chains that reset to zero.** These convert intrinsic motivation into an extrinsic metric, and a single lapse triggers the abstinence-violation / "what-the-hell" effect → shame → abandonment. Multiple reviews and app designers document higher dropout for all-or-nothing streaks. Notably, Duolingo's Retention Team ran over 600 experiments on the Streak feature alone over four years; separating the daily goal from the streak produced a 3.3% increase in Day-14 retention and, within 20 days, a 10.5% increase in the percentage of daily learners on a streak — evidence that *de-fanging* streak anxiety improves retention. Anchor's rolling-window/total-count tracking is the right call. Note Lally et al. (2010, UCL, 96 participants) found median time to automaticity was 66 days (range 18–254) and that *"missing one opportunity to perform the behavior did not materially affect the habit formation process"* — so a single missed day is behaviorally irrelevant and should never be punished. Heed Finch's documented caveat, though: removing *all* consequences can reduce accountability for some users, so Anchor should still surface *progress* (trajectory / percentage-of-days), just never a punitive zero.
2. **Punitive gamification (Habitica-style HP loss / character death).** Habitica damages the player for missed Dailies — compounded in boss quests where it hits the whole party, and culminating in character "death" (losing a level, all gold, and equipment). Users themselves file complaints about disproportionate damage. This is actively harmful for depression-adjacent users; avoid any loss/decay mechanic tied to inactivity.
3. **Trend-driven, high-cognitive-load UI.** Per the July 2026 Smashing Magazine analysis ("Designing For Distressed Users," Kat Homan), mental-health apps see almost 95% of day-1 users abandon by day 30, with a median 30-day retention of only 3.3%, and even recognised giants losing ~50% of users within the first ten days. Flashy animations, novel gestures, and unconventional icons add cognitive load "when the user can least afford it." The design test: *does each element lower the cost of using the app in a low-capacity state?*
4. **Overfilling the timeline.** Tiimo's own guidance warns that a visual schedule "can become just as oppressive as a written list" when it contains every micro-task — a direct caution for Anchor's "make rest visible" thesis: render rest as *legitimacy*, not as one more thing to complete.
5. **Clinical/diagnostic framing and stigmatizing language.** Destigmatizing-design research (a Delphi-study guideline set) concludes developers should focus not just on technical solutions but on content-related and emotional design that can inadvertently aggravate stigma. The KRIYA study found that neutral, non-judgmental language "lowered the barrier to engaging with potentially discouraging data," with users interpreting low values as information rather than personal failure. Keep Anchor non-clinical: no PHQ-style labels, no "symptoms," empathetic microcopy, and constructive (never blaming) miss states.

### (d) Gaps & opportunities — where Anchor can be genuinely new
- **The within-day path/transit metaphor is whitespace.** Prior art exists for (i) subway-map metaphors applied to multi-week *project* schedules (Burkhard & Meier, "Tube Map Visualization," *J. Universal Computer Science*, 2005, where activities are "train lines" and milestones are "stations"; and Stott et al., "Automatic layout of project plans using a metro map metaphor," IV05, 2005), and (ii) long-term "journey/voyage/trail" gamified *progress* maps (Fabulous "Journeys," ErgoLife's sea-voyage across islands, Finch quests). But no shipping consumer app renders a *single day* as a continuous spatial path where each task is a station with a start time and duration. The nearest name-match — Crush Entropy's "Trail" — is a scheduling/progress timeline, not a spatial path map. This is genuine novelty.
- **Rest-as-first-class visible segment is under-explored and under-studied.** Timeline apps (Tiimo, Structured, Fhynix) *allow* rest as an optional block, and Owaves' circadian 24-hour clock renders sleep/rest/meals as first-class — but none enforce rest as a first-class, non-completable object, and no behavioral study isolates "visible rest segment vs. blank gap" as the manipulation. Anchor can pioneer here — and potentially publish.
- **Never-forced momentum stack** differs from Fabulous's sequential auto-advancing routines (which push one ritual after another with a countdown). The autonomy-preserving "offer, don't force" design is more consistent with self-determination theory and with the low-motivation audience.
- **Caution from HCI evidence:** metro-map metaphors can overwhelm as complexity rises (documented risks include complexity and "absorptive capacity"), and at least one clinical "journey" app (PMC7899805) scored high on usability (SUS ~85) yet *did not* improve users' motivation to manage their health. So the path must stay visually simple and be validated to actually lower start-friction, not merely look novel.

## Details

**Existing apps surveyed.** *Finch* (self-care pet; complete self-care activities to earn energy that sends the bird on adventures; no-penalty tracking with guilt-free streak pause; themed quests; CBT-flavored exercises; generous free tier, Plus $29.99/yr) is the tone benchmark. *Habitica* (RPG with HP loss and character death for missed Dailies) is the anti-pattern for this audience. *Fabulous* (born 2013 out of Duke's Behavioral Economics Lab; "Journeys," rituals, habit-stacking, and sequential auto-advancing routines with countdown timers and audio coaching; ~37M users) is the closest "guided routine" model but uses long-term journey framing and can auto-push. *Owaves* (physician-designed 24-hour circadian "sunrise clock" that renders sleep/rest/meals as first-class; BodyClock AI; 1.3M+ downloads) is the closest to "rest as first-class" and "whole-day visualization," though its metaphor is a radial clock, not a path. *Tiimo* (neurodivergent-first visual timeline; AI breakdown; gentle prompts; 500k+ users, NHS-referenced) and *Structured* (proportional vertical timeline; 1.5M+ users, 400k+ ratings) are the visual-day-planner benchmarks. Digital BA apps (*Moodivate, MoodKit, Actify!, Maummove, B-ACT*) supply the clinical evidence base. Newer 2025–26 entrants (Kriya, Habi, HabitPath, and various "no-streak" trackers) are converging on trajectory/pattern-based tracking over streaks.

**Behavioral science.** Implementation intentions: Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) d=.65 on goal attainment, d=.77 on preventing derailment; Sheeran/Listrom/Gollwitzer (2024, 642 tests) .27–.66. Tiny Habits: Fogg B=MAP + celebration; Lally et al. (2010) median automaticity 66 days (range 18–254), single misses immaterial. BA/activity scheduling: Cuijpers et al. (2007) d=0.87, equivalent to cognitive therapy. Streak psychology: abstinence-violation effect, "what-the-hell" abandonment, and the intrinsic→extrinsic motivation shift (self-determination theory). Pacing: energy-envelope model, pre-emptive vs. recuperative rest, boom-bust avoidance.

**mHealth engagement.** DMHI dropout is severe: Torous/Firth's meta-analysis of 18 RCTs found a pooled dropout of 26.2%, rising to 47.8% after adjusting for publication bias; Linardon et al. (2020, *J. Consult. Clin. Psychol.*) is the broader attrition/adherence review; and a 2025 update (Liu, Torous, Fuller-Tyszkiewicz et al., *JAMA Psychiatry*, 79 trials) found attrition was lower in trials that offered reminders, human contact, and *no* gamification features. An emerging design principle in the narrative literature: deliver a *minimally effective dose fast* rather than assume more engagement is always better; deep participatory adaptation and human support correlate with lower dropout.

**Language/framing.** Destigmatizing-design Delphi guidelines emphasize content-and-emotional design (not just technical fixes); neutral non-judgmental language (KRIYA study) lowers the barrier to engaging with discouraging data.

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — MVP core (build first):**
1. Implement the locked-night-before path as an explicit if-then plan: each station stores a cue (time) + response (action) + duration. Make rest stations first-class and non-completable — they exist to legitimize recovery, not to be checked off.
2. Ship anti-streak tracking as a rolling-window completion rate / trajectory line from day one; never display a zero or a broken chain. Include a Finch-style "return-friendly" state that welcomes users back after a gap.
3. Non-clinical language pass on all copy: no diagnostic terms, constructive miss-states, empathetic microcopy. Establish a banned-words list (no "symptoms," "failure," "streak lost").
4. Physical-anchor bias: default the protected "least-bad-hour" slot to a physical/starter task (cooking, a short walk) that yields visible proof of progress.

**Stage 2 — differentiators:**
5. Build the never-forced momentum stack as an *offer* card on completion, dismissible with zero friction and no negative consequence for declining.
6. Add optional immediate celebration on completion (Tiny Habits "Shine").
7. Add AI/manual task breakdown for tasks that feel too big to start.

**Stage 3 — validate & (optionally) publish:**
8. Run a small within-subjects test isolating the novel claims: (a) visible rest segment vs. blank gap — does it reduce guilt and increase adherence? (b) path metaphor vs. vertical timeline — does it lower start-friction? Both are genuine, unfilled research gaps.

**Benchmarks that would change the plan:**
- If early testing shows the path metaphor increases cognitive load or confusion (a documented risk of metro-map metaphors as complexity rises), fall back to a Structured-style vertical timeline with rest blocks.
- If no-consequence tracking reduces engagement (Finch's documented weakness), add *positive* progress salience (trajectory, gentle "fresh start" prompts) — never punitive resets.
- If 30-day retention tracks the category's dismal ~3.3% baseline, prioritize a fast minimally-effective-dose onboarding over feature depth, and add reminders/light human contact (associated with lower attrition in the 2025 JAMA Psychiatry analysis).

## Caveats
- Much app-feature detail comes from vendor sites and review roundups (marketing-adjacent); verify actual UI hands-on before committing. The strongest sources are peer-reviewed: Gollwitzer & Sheeran; Cuijpers et al.; the JMIR/Frontiers BA trials; Lally et al.; and Burkhard & Meier.
- Effect sizes (d=.65, d=0.87) come from meta-analyses of clinical and goal-attainment contexts, not from a consumer app identical to Anchor; treat as directional, not guaranteed.
- The "starting one task lifts capacity to start another" premise is well-supported as activation-energy / behavioral-momentum in general and ABA populations, but I found no study testing it specifically in seasonal-low-mood/depression-adjacent users via an app momentum-prompt; Anchor should validate it.
- Some cited blog/vendor statistics (e.g., "47% more likely to binge," "reduce procrastination by 35%") are single-source or marketing claims and should not be treated as established.
- No behavioral study isolates "rest rendered as a visible segment vs. blank gap," so that specific claim is currently a hypothesis, not evidence — which is exactly why it represents Anchor's clearest opportunity to contribute something new.
